If you have ads running on Facebook or Instagram, what happens after someone taps them may have changed this fall. You didn't build a new campaign or flip a switch. According to a Meta advertiser notice reported by trade press, product browsing was set to turn on for existing ads on October 28. That was 12 days ago, and Black Friday is close behind. Here's what the feature does and how to check your ads.

## What product browsing actually is

Product browsing is one of the creative enhancements Meta offers through Advantage+ creative. Meta's [Help Center page on Advantage+ creative](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/297506218282224?locale=en_US) describes it as a personalized browsing experience that can show product details, offers and more on Facebook and Instagram when someone taps the image or video in your ad. From there, the page says, people are led to your website to make purchases.

So the sale still happens on your site. What can change is the path to it. The same page says this enhancement may send people to an experience on Facebook and Instagram first, before your website. Notice the word "may." Not every tap is promised to open the in-app view.

This is a different subject from the feed controls we covered in [our post on Facebook's "Your Algorithm"](/blog/facebook-your-algorithm-el-paso-business-posts/). That one was about what people see in their feeds. This one is about what happens after they tap your ad.

## What trade press says changed on October 28

The feature isn't new. The reported change is to the default. An advertiser notice reproduced by [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/meta-will-turn-on-product-browsing-in-existing-ads-on-october-28/) on October 15 quotes Meta saying that starting October 28, 2026, it would add product browsing to your existing ads. As reported, the notice says people who tap your ad may see an experience on Facebook or Instagram with product info and personalized suggestions, meant to help them learn about your products quickly. [Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/meta/meta-product-browsing-existing-ads-october-28/) covered the same notice on October 16, working from PPC Land's copy.

Our team found no Meta Help Center article, newsroom post or changelog that announces the October 28 switch, and PPC Land said it couldn't find one either. Meta's Help page describes the feature but doesn't mention this date. So everything about the switch comes from the notice as trade press reported it.

The notice also leaves gaps. As PPC Land reported it, the notice doesn't name the campaign objectives, ad formats or placements involved, and it doesn't say whether ads created after October 28 start with product browsing on. Those questions are still open.

## How to check and control it now

As reported, the notice offered two ways to say no. The first was an "Opt out" button, which the notice said to use before October 28. The notice doesn't say whether that button covered one ad account or a whole business portfolio. The second route was described as available anytime. According to the notice, you can turn product browsing on or off anytime in an ad's Advantage+ Creative and Destination settings.

Is it on in your account right now? We haven't looked inside your Ads Manager, so we can't tell you. If the change rolled out as the notice described and nobody on your team used the opt-out button, it may well be on. [GoodMorningCo](https://goodmorningco.com/blog/meta-ads-product-browsing-default), writing on October 28, stated that it would be live that day for advertisers who hadn't opted out. That's their reading of the notice, not a statement from Meta.

We suggest a simple check. Open your active Facebook and Instagram ads and look at the product browsing setting in each ad's Advantage+ Creative and Destination settings, starting with the ads that carry the most budget. Then check any ad created since October 28, because the notice is silent on new ads. One more caution. Meta's Help page does say some enhancements may be on by default and can be turned off at any time. That line is about Advantage+ creative in general. It isn't Meta confirming the October 28 change.

## Why this matters before Black Friday

Black Friday falls on November 27, 30 days after the reported switch date. In our reading, the next few weeks carry some of the most important ad spend of the year for many small shops, and the risk isn't that product browsing is bad. It's that the path may have changed without anyone on your side choosing it. If your landing page carries the holiday offer, your hours, a gift-wrap note or the bilingual message you wrote, you want to know whether shoppers reach it before they decide.

It may also affect how you read results. David Herrmann, a media buyer quoted by PPC Land, objected to the timing and said that in client accounts, results credited to a creative actually came from an in-app experience Meta built around it. PPC Land said it couldn't verify those cases, so treat them as one buyer's account, not evidence. They're still a reason to check before holiday numbers arrive.

## What the 3% conversion claim does and doesn't tell you

The notice, as reproduced by PPC Land, says businesses saw 3% more conversions on average with in-app product browsing than campaigns without it. A footnote ties that to a Meta experiment run August 6 to 21, 2026, across about 117,700 advertisers globally, in three or more verticals including e-commerce, CPG and retail, with what Meta called more than 99% confidence.

That's Meta's own number, and we only have it secondhand. The notice, as reproduced, doesn't say which conversion was counted, what attribution window applied, how the average was worked out, or what happened to cost or return on ad spend. In our reading, more conversions don't tell you much if each one costs more. We also found no figures for El Paso, Juárez or Mexico businesses, so we wouldn't treat 3% as the lift a typical small business should expect.

Our suggestion is to judge it on your own orders, meaning the sales your register, booking system or online store actually records, not on Ads Manager conversions alone.

## Practical next steps for El Paso and borderplex owners

We suggest one short audit this week, not a rebuild.

1. **List your active Meta ads** and check the product browsing setting on each one. Write down what you find.
2. **Ask what your landing page does.** If the page does the selling, with a specific offer or a bilingual explanation, you may prefer taps to go straight there. If shoppers mostly browse products, leaving it on and watching may be reasonable.
3. **Watch site traffic against reported clicks.** If Ads Manager shows steady clicks but visits to your site from Meta drop, that gap deserves a closer look.

Picture a hypothetical Juárez gift shop whose holiday ad points to a Spanish-language page with its pickup hours and wrapping options. That page is doing real work, so the owner might turn product browsing off on that ad. A hypothetical Eastside El Paso store with lots of similar items might leave it on and compare. Either choice can be right. What matters is making it on purpose.
