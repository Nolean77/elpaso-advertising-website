If you run Google Ads in the U.S., your campaigns may already be eligible for a placement you never chose. You didn't build a new campaign or set a new budget for it. Our take is that this matters more to your account than the list of new countries in Google's latest announcement.

## What Google announced on Oct 28

On October 28, 2026, [Google said](https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/ads-in-ai-mode-global/) it is scaling ads in AI Mode in 10 more countries: Australia, Canada, India, Kenya, Malaysia, New Zealand, Nigeria, Pakistan, the Philippines and Singapore. Google also says it plans to expand in Q4 2026 to more locales, including Brazil, Mexico and the United Kingdom.

The other piece is a U.S. launch called multiturn ads, which allow ads on follow-up questions. A Google spokesperson's description, [reported by Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-expands-ads-ai-mode-42412.html), says these ads appear on responses to follow-ups as people go back and forth with AI Mode. So an ad is no longer tied only to the first thing someone asks.

Google says the ads will be labeled "Sponsored." Its [help page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/18826097) also says paid advertising does not influence the organic responses, so buying ads doesn't change what the AI says about you. That unpaid side is a different topic, and we covered it in [our post on AI search](/blog/AI-Search-Is-Quietly-Changing-Where-El-Paso-Customers-Find-Your-Business-in-2026/).

## Which Google Ads campaigns are eligible

According to Google's help page, advertisers don't need new campaigns or separate budgets. Existing Search, Shopping and Performance Max campaigns are automatically eligible to serve ads in AI Overviews and AI Mode. For now, the page says this is available in English in the United States and select English-speaking markets.

Google's [help page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/18826097) confirms that advertisers can't target AI Mode or AI Overviews exclusively or opt out of them separately from Search. Negative keywords still apply.

Our take is that eligible doesn't mean your ads are showing there, and it doesn't mean they aren't. Since Google doesn't ask for a separate budget, any spend in AI Mode comes out of the money you've already set aside. It's worth knowing what your campaigns are now allowed to do.

## Match types help decide whether you show up in conversations

This is the setting we'd look at first. Google's help page says AI Max, broad match, Shopping and Performance Max are eligible for contextual matching. Exact and phrase match keywords aren't eligible for contextual matching on broader exploratory questions.

In plain terms, the way you built a campaign affects how far it can reach into those wider questions.

Our take is that this isn't a reason to rush into broad match, and it isn't a reason to abandon it either. For a small account, we'd suggest going campaign by campaign and writing down its type and match types. Then ask one question. Do you want this budget reaching broader, exploratory questions, or only the direct searches you built it for?

Either answer can be right. Picture a hypothetical El Paso plumbing company with one modest Search campaign. It might want exact and phrase match on urgent repair terms, because every dollar has a job to do. Another business with more room to test might accept broader reach on purpose. The mistake is ending up with either setup by accident. And since Google's help page says you can't opt out of AI Mode separately from Search, keep in mind that a match type change affects that campaign everywhere it runs, not just in AI Mode.

## What you can and can't measure

Google's help page says these results are "included in aggregate" in standard reporting and in the search terms report, although Google omits some low-volume queries. So your AI Mode results are blended in with the rest. Nothing we read from Google describes a separate AI Mode report, so don't build a plan that depends on one.

Our take is that the search terms report is still worth a regular read. Look for long, conversational queries and ask whether you wanted to pay for them. A long query doesn't prove a click came from AI Mode, so treat it as a clue. If you see terms that don't fit, such as a hypothetical roofing company paying for do-it-yourself repair questions, add negative keywords.

## Landing pages for longer questions

Google says queries in AI Mode are, on average, three times longer than traditional searches. That's Google's own figure for AI Mode overall, not a number for your industry or your town. Google also says click-through rates are already similar between sponsored and organic product list items in AI Mode. That one covers product list items only, so a service business shouldn't borrow it.

Our take is that someone who asks a longer question has usually told you more about what they need. If your ad then drops them on a general homepage, they have to start over. We'd suggest checking where each ad actually sends people. A hypothetical Eastside auto shop running a brake repair ad should land visitors on a page about brake repair, with its hours and service area easy to find, not on a page that lists everything it does.

## Mexico and cross-border advertisers, what's known and what isn't

Here's what's known. Google says it plans to expand ads in AI Mode to Mexico in Q4 2026, and Search Engine Roundtable reported the same plan. "Next" in our headline means next on Google's stated list. It doesn't mean a launch you can schedule around.

Here's what isn't known. Google hasn't given a date. The help page says English only for now, and nothing we read says Spanish-language ads will run in Mexico. PPC Land notes that Google's post doesn't say whether ads in Mexico will run in Spanish.

Our take, if you advertise on both sides of the border, is not to rebuild anything around a plan with no date. Picture a hypothetical Juárez business with a campaign aimed at customers in Mexico. We'd suggest noting which campaigns target Mexico and which match types they use, so you can respond quickly if Google publishes details. Until then, we'd leave those campaigns as they are.

## What not to assume, and what to do next

Don't assume this placement will help your business. The sources we reviewed offer no figures for local businesses. Don't assume you'll be able to separate AI Mode results in your reports. And don't assume that Mexico's expansion will look like the U.S. version.

We'd start with three checks. See which campaigns use broad match, AI Max or Performance Max, and how much they're spending. Read your search terms and tighten your negative keywords where needed. Then recheck Google's help page from time to time, because that's where we'd expect to see any change to the language or market details.
