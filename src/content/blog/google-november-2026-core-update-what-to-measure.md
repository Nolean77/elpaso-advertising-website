Several times a year Google makes broad changes to how it ranks search results. The November 2026 core update is the latest one. Before you rewrite a page or worry over one slow week, it helps to know what Google actually said, what it didn't, and when your numbers are worth reading.

## What Google officially confirmed about the November 2026 core update

Google started the November 2026 core update on November 10, 2026, at 10:06 a.m. Pacific time. That's 11:06 a.m. Mountain for those of us in El Paso. Its Search Status Dashboard says the update "applies globally and to all languages" and adds, "The rollout may take up to 2 weeks to complete."

That second part is a window, not a deadline. Google had not announced a completion date on the dashboard at our November 19 check. When we checked the dashboard on the evening of November 19, it still listed the update as Active. It may have changed since, so open the [Search Status Dashboard](https://status.search.google.com/) and check whether it says Active or complete.

In a LinkedIn post quoted by Search Engine Roundtable, Google called this "a regular update designed to better surface relevant, satisfying content for searchers from all types of sites." Google's standing documentation adds that core updates "are broad in nature, and don't target specific sites or individual web pages." Google's Ranking history lists two earlier 2026 updates titled "core update," in March and May, plus one titled "February 2026 Discover update."

What Google didn't say matters too. It didn't name which industries or page types will gain or lose, and it didn't call this a penalty.

## Why this matters even if you mostly care about Google Maps

Google's Business Profile help page says "Local results are mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity." Under the heading Prominence, it says that factor is "also based on info like how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have."

What follows is our reasoning, not something Google said about this update. Your website and your Business Profile aren't two separate worlds. A site that earns links and serves people well supports the kind of reputation Google describes as prominence. So even if most of your calls come from Maps, your website is still worth watching during a core update.

## What to do while the dashboard still says Active

If the dashboard still shows Active, patience is usually the right move. Google notes that positions in Search aren't fixed and that constant change "can cause both gains and drops in organic Search traffic." Its guidance also points away from reacting fast. In Google's words, "Avoid doing 'quick fix' changes (like removing some page element because you heard it was bad for SEO). Instead, focus on making changes that make sense for your users and are sustainable in the long term."

That isn't a ban on touching your site. Google's guidance doesn't ask for a freeze, and in our view fixing a wrong phone number or posting updated hours is ordinary upkeep. The thing to avoid is tearing pages apart over a few days of movement.

If the dashboard already says the rollout is complete, skip ahead to the next section.

## When and how to check your numbers after it finishes

Google's core updates page gives a clear order. First, "Confirm that the core update has finished rolling out." Then wait. "We recommend waiting at least a full week after a core update completes before analyzing your site in Search Console." After that week, Google suggests "comparing this week with a week before the core update started rolling out."

Applying that to this update is our own date math, not a Google sentence. Since the update began on November 10, your "before" week is the week before November 10. Your "after" week comes once at least a full week has passed since the dashboard marked the update complete.

In Search Console, compare those two weeks for your top pages and top queries. A small drop, like going from position 2 to 4, doesn't call for drastic action. A large drop, like going from position 4 to 29, calls for a deeper assessment. Google also suggests checking Web Search, Google Images, Video mode, and the News tab separately so you know where a change happened.

What to ignore, in our view, follows from the same sources. Ignore the daily swings during the rollout. Ignore anyone who treats Google's rollout window as a guaranteed finish date. And ignore claims that this update "targets" a type of business or works like a penalty, because Google says core updates don't target specific sites.

## What "relevant, satisfying content" looks like for a small site

Google's November 10 announcement did not include a checklist for this update. Its self-assessment guidance asks whether your site is delivering content that's "helpful, reliable, and people first." Here's our read for a small business.

- Service pages that say plainly what you do, where you do it, and who it's for.
- Accurate details like hours, address, phone, and service area that match your Business Profile.
- Answers to the questions customers already ask you on the phone.
- Pages written for people instead of padded with keywords.

If you're considering deleting entire sections of your site, Google's page says "that's likely a sign those sections were created for search engines first, and not people."

Picture a hypothetical moving company serving both El Paso and Juárez. A clear English page and a clear Spanish page for its cross-border service, each answering the real questions customers ask, is the kind of people-first content we mean. That example is ours, not a Google recommendation.

## What's documented about the local pack, and what isn't

Google's Business Profile help page explains local ranking in general terms, under the headings Relevance, Distance, and Prominence. It doesn't mention this core update. The core update pages we read don't say this update changes Map Pack rankings either.

That isn't proof local results are untouched. It only means the sources we read didn't say so. If your map position changes around the rollout, a connection to this update isn't confirmed.

## Simple next steps for El Paso and borderplex businesses

1. Check the Search Status Dashboard to see whether the update is still Active or complete.
2. Write down a baseline. Save your Search Console numbers for the week before November 10 and list the pages that matter most to your business.
3. If Maps drives your calls, note where you show up today for your main services.
4. If the update is still Active, wait, and skip the quick fixes.
5. Once it's complete, wait at least a full week, then compare. Only then decide whether anything needs work.
